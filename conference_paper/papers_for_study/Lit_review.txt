\outl0\strokewidth0 \strokec2 Recent literature on Security Operations Center (SOC) optimization underscores a heavy reliance on automated alert triage to mitigate analyst alert fatigue and prolonged response metrics. Prior studies establish that traditional rule-based Security Information and Event Management (SIEM) engines generate substantial volumes of false positives, necessitating advanced machine learning models to improve filtering efficacy. Several researchers have explored supervised classifiers\'97such as Random Forest, Support Vector Machines, and Extreme Gradient Boosting\'97to automate anomaly detection and classification tasks.\
\
Concurrently, academic and industry frameworks have increasingly emphasized the integration of contextual risk metrics into threat prioritization pipelines. While purely technical models evaluate anomalies based on signature frequency or severity scores, emerging research highlights the necessity of incorporating asset criticality, organizational dependencies, and potential business impact to contextualize threats accurately. Furthermore, recent work in explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) addresses the inherent "black box" limitation of complex machine learning classifiers, ensuring that security analysts can interpret model outputs during high-pressure triage workflows. Despite these advancements, a gap remains in seamlessly coupling probabilistic machine learning outputs with comprehensive business risk models, highlighting the need for hybrid architectures that bridge technical detection accuracy with enterprise-level risk assessment.\
